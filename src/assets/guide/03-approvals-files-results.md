# Approvals, files and results

A chat asks things of you and hands things back. What it asks comes as a **card**: only you can answer it, and the work waits for your answer. This chapter is about the kinds of card, and about the files a chat takes and gives.

## Approvals

An agent's call waits for your approval when its level is above the chat's trust level, or when your organization's policy says that function always asks. The card names the agent and the function, and its level, and, for a call on a record, which one.

**Allow**, and the call goes on; **Don't allow**, and the agent is told, and the assistant works around it or stops. You may also have your answer remembered, for that agent, function and record: the next call like it does not ask. Every approval you remembered is listed on the agent's page under **Agents**, where you can take it back.

A chat with a card waiting is marked in the chat list, and you can be notified on your devices or by email, so a decision does not wait for you to look: **Settings → Notifications**.

## When an agent asks

An agent may need something only you can give:

- **A question**, with words, a table or a chart to look at; you answer in your own words, pick one of its choices, or attach a file, as it asks — or decline it, and the agent goes on without an answer.
- **A sign-in**: a secret it keeps for you has fields nobody filled in — a password, the answers a site's form wants — or an account to connect with its provider. What you type goes into the secret, never onto the card, and the assistant never sees it. An answer the agent says not to remember is handed to it once and kept nowhere.
- **Reaching a site** it did not declare: the card shows the hosts, what for, and the code that needs them. A yes opens those hosts for that one call.

The agent waits while you decide, and its time limit does not run meanwhile. A question nobody answers within a day is answered with nothing, and the agent goes on without it. Stopping the chat closes its cards.

## Offers

The assistant itself may offer to remember a fact, or to save a way of working as a skill. The card shows it word for word; it is kept only if you say **Save**.

## What comes back

The assistant's words are its own. Beside them may be:

- **Files** an agent made — a document, a workbook, a statement — to open or download. They are yours, under **Data → Files**.
- **What an agent showed you**: its own words, a table or a chart, under its name.

Treat a claim in the assistant's words as a claim: what ran is in the chat's work as it happened, and on the audit trail.

## Attachments

Attach a file to a message — from this device, or one you already keep — and it goes with your words. A file you upload is your own; an agent reaches it only when the assistant hands it to a call, visibly, in the chat. An agent never changes an original: what it makes is a new file.
