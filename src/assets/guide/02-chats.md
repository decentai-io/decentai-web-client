# Chats

A chat is a conversation with the assistant and the agents it may use. It is yours alone: nobody else sees it. This chapter is about asking, following the work, and a chat's own settings.

## Your chats

**AI → Chats** opens on a box to type in: write there to start a new chat. Beneath it are your earlier chats, newest first, each saying whether it is working and how many cards wait in it for you.

A chat can be renamed, archived — put out of the way, and restored from the archived ones — cleared of its conversation, or deleted, which takes its messages, cards, schedules and the files attached to it.

## Asking

Say what you want in plain words, with the facts the work needs: names, dates, amounts, which notebook, which customer. The assistant reads what each of the chat's agents can do, and calls their functions in the order the work needs.

Good asks name the outcome, not the steps:

> Find Harbourline's request in my mailbox, compare the three chair quotations we received, and tell me which meets our requirements.

When it lacks a fact it needs, the assistant asks you rather than guessing. Files you attach go with your words; they are yours, and reach an agent only when the assistant hands one to a call.

## What it is doing

While the assistant works, the last line of the chat says what it is doing: thinking, running which agent's function, pausing, summarizing earlier messages, or waiting for you, and, past half the turn's budget, which step it is on. Each call and task shows in the turn's work as it starts and as it ends: running, done, failed or stopped, with how long it took, and an agent's latest line of progress. Open a row for its whole story: why it ran, what it was given, what came back (the whole of it with **Open the whole output**), the files it returned, the records it touched, and whether it asked you. A task's own calls are under it. While the assistant writes its answer, the words appear as they come; the message is kept once it is whole, with the model that wrote it, its calls and how long it took under it.

A schedule's or a webhook's run is a block of its own, with its work and what it said. What happened besides the conversation, a schedule added or run, work stopped, a setting you changed, is a quiet line of its own, at its time. All of it is there again when you come back to the chat.

**About this chat**, the **i** in the chat's bar, opens a panel beside the conversation: what runs now, each with its own stop; the chat's schedules and when each runs next; the decisions you chose to remember for its agents, each to forget (they are yours, so they apply in every chat); and its setup.

An agent can speak in the chat itself: words, a table, a chart, or choices. What it shows is the agent's, under its name; what the assistant says is the assistant's.

An agent that drives a screen, a browser above all, can show it in the chat as it goes. You can take it over — for a sign-in or a CAPTCHA a site wants a human for — click and type on it, and hand it back.

## Memory and skills

The assistant may offer to remember a fact, or to save a way of working as a skill; each comes as a card, and is kept only if you say so. What it remembers is under **Settings → Memory**, yours to correct or delete; skills are under **Data → Skills**.

## Schedules from a chat

Ask for something *on Friday morning* or *every Monday at 9* and the assistant puts it on the clock: a prompt that wakes it then, or an agent's function made to run with nobody present. It says so in the chat, and every schedule is on **AI → Schedules**.

## The chat's own settings

Each chat has its own:

- **Trust level**, 0 to 3: how far its agents may act without asking.
- **Agents**: every agent you may use, those installed later included, or only those you choose — none, if you like.
- **Model** and **effort**: the model its assistant thinks with, picked among the models of the connections you may use, and how hard; none chosen is the organization's default.
- **Time zone**: what "Friday 9 am" means to its schedules.
- **Turn budget**, and the **skills** and **MCP servers** its assistant uses: every one, or those you choose.

What a new chat starts with is under **Settings → Chat defaults**.

What a new chat starts with is yours to set under **Settings → Chat defaults**.

A message can also be spoken, where your organization set speech up: what you say is written into the box for you to send. A reply can be read aloud the same way.

## Stopping

**Stop**, in a chat, stops what that chat is doing: its calls are cancelled, and the cards waiting in it close. A running call or task in a turn's work has a stop of its own: it stops alone, and the rest goes on.

**Stop everything**, at the top of every page, stops every chat and schedule of yours at once, and holds them stopped: nothing of yours starts again until you press **Resume**. It stops your own work only, and what was already done stays done. An administrator can stop everything of a person too.
